mixture of kittens

Mixture-of-experts (MoE) training megakernel for NVL72s

Forked from cursor/mixture-of-kittens

The Problem

Standard Mixture-of-Experts training suffers from serialized compute and communication steps, frequent CPU-GPU synchronizations, and non-deterministic execution. These overheads compound on NVL72 systems where inter-GPU communication latency directly impacts training throughput. Existing solutions leave significant headroom on the table.

What This Does

Mixture-of-Kittens fuses all MoE computation and communication into a single CUDA megakernel (csrc/mok_megakernel.cuh). The kernel overlaps compute and inter-GPU networking at configurable granularity, eliminating CPU-GPU synchronization entirely. It supports both BF16 and MXFP8 precision for forward and backward passes.

The library exposes two APIs: a low-level ops layer (mok/ops.py) for direct kernel calls, and a functional layer (mok/functional.py) that manages scratch memory and kernel launch coordination. The functional layer is the production recommendation—call schedule() once to build the dispatch/combine schedule, then reuse it across forward() and backward() calls. Five hyperparameters control communication SM allocation and must be tuned per workload.

Real-World Use

This powers production training of Composer at Cursor. Standalone benchmarks show 1.58-2.37x speedups over the fastest baselines across both precisions and passes. The benchmarks/ directory contains reproducible benchmarks for MoE layers, NCCL operations, and hybrid EP comparisons.

The Bottom Line

A serious, production-tested MoE kernel that delivers real performance gains on NVL72 hardware. The hardware requirements (Blackwell GPUs, CUDA 13.0) make it irrelevant for most teams today. If you run MoE training on NVL72s and need maximum throughput, this is worth evaluating—but expect to invest in build reproducibility and CI before relying on it in production.
