BigLaw

AI for legal work.

Forked from discover-legal/BigLaw

The Problem

Legal technology at Am Law 100 firms costs $50,000–$150,000 per lawyer per year. Small firms and solo practitioners cannot access these tools. Most legal AI projects focus on narrow use cases—document review or contract analysis—and leave the broader workflow (drafting, billing, docketing, collaboration) unaddressed.

What This Does

BigLaw is a single Go binary that replicates the core legal tech stack as an open-source platform. The codebase at biglaw-go/internal/ contains 172 Go files covering research, drafting, redlining, e-signatures, briefing, docketing, billing, and collaboration. It runs on a Raspberry Pi with 4GB RAM or entirely on local models via Ollama/LM Studio.

The system orchestrates 100+ agents through a structured multi-round debate architecture defined in biglaw-go/internal/agents/. The topoflow/ directory implements a topology-based routing and governance layer. internal/lpm/ handles the legal practice management pipeline—intake, drafting, portfolio management, and reporting. External integrations include Clio (internal/integrations/clio.go), Slack and Teams bots (internal/bots/), and DocuSeal for e-signatures (internal/tools/docuseal.go).

Real-World Use

A solo practitioner could deploy the Go binary, configure a local LLM via internal/providers/ollama.go, and run the full intake-to-billing workflow from a single server. The docker-compose.yml in biglaw-go/ provides a one-command deployment. The benchmarks/harvey-lab/ directory contains a Python-based evaluation harness for comparing model performance across legal tasks.

Low - Mixed language stack: 7 Python files in benchmarks/ create a secondary dependency chain (requirements.txt) for a Go-centric project. Not a defect, but adds maintenance surface.

The Bottom Line

BigLaw is the most ambitious open legal AI platform available, with genuine breadth across legal workflows and a thoughtful agent architecture. It is not production-ready—the security posture requires hardening, and the experimental status is honest, not modest. Suited for firms with engineering capability who want to evaluate or build on an open legal AI stack, but not for firms expecting a drop-in replacement for commercial tools.
