The Problem

Generating 3D CAD models programmatically is difficult because most deep generative models operate on meshes or voxels, not on the construction sequences that CAD software actually uses. Engineers need a way to generate valid, editable CAD models that preserve the original design intent—not just shape approximations.

What This Does

DeepCAD is an ICCV 2021 paper implementation that learns to generate CAD models as construction sequences. The pipeline uses a two-stage approach: an autoencoder (model/autoencoder.py) compresses CAD sequences into a latent space, then a latent GAN (model/latentGAN.py) generates novel sequences from random noise. The vectorized representation is defined in cadlib/macro.py and handles the core CAD operations—extrusions, sketches, and boolean operations.

Data flows from Onshape JSON files through dataset/json2vec.py for training, and results export to STEP format via utils/export2step.py. The trainer/ directory separates training logic for the autoencoder (trainerAE.py) and the latent GAN (trainerLGAN.py), with loss functions in trainer/loss.py.

Real-World Use

A mechanical design team could train DeepCAD on their library of existing CAD models, then use the trained generator to produce novel part variations for concept exploration. The output is a construction sequence, not a mesh, so engineers can open the result in Onshape or SolidWorks and edit parameters directly.

The Bottom Line

DeepCAD is a well-structured research codebase that solves a real problem—generating CAD construction sequences rather than meshes. The autoencoder + latent GAN architecture is cleanly separated, and the evaluation scripts (evaluation/) provide meaningful metrics (Chamfer distance, command accuracy). It is suitable for researchers or teams who need to extend generative CAD work, but the lack of tests and CI makes it risky as a production dependency without significant hardening.
