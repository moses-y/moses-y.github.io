videosos

Enable AI models for video production in the browser

Forked from timoncool/videosos

The Problem Video production has traditionally been a resource-intensive task, often requiring hefty software and cloud services, leading to privacy concerns and high costs. For many creators, the lack of a straightforward, browser-based solution that handles AI video generation and editing is a major pain point.

What This Does Enter VideoSOS, an open-source video editor designed to run entirely in your browser. The project leverages AI models for text-to-video, image generation, and audio creation without the hassle of uploads or privacy invasions. The core of the app relies on FFmpeg.wasm and Remotion, found in the Makefile and various scripts, to handle video rendering locally.

The README.md outlines how to get started, while INSTALL-PORTABLE.txt gives you the lowdown on setting up your environment. Want to track project costs? The VIDEOMODELSPARAMETERS.md file ensures you know how much you're spending on each media item.

Real-World Use Imagine you're a content creator needing to whip up a quick promotional video. You open VideoSOS, select a text-to-video model like Google Veo 3.1, and type in your script. The timeline editor lets you drag and drop elements, adjust audio tracks, and fine-tune visuals—all while keeping an eye on your budget with the cost tracking feature. You export your project directly in the browser, avoiding the waiting game of cloud processing.

Here's a simple snippet to kick off a new project:

const videoProject = new VideoProject(); videoProject.addClip('intro.mp4'); videoProject.addAudio('background-music.mp3'); videoProject.render();

The Bottom Line VideoSOS is a solid pick for anyone looking to produce videos without the bloat of traditional software. It's especially useful for small to medium projects where privacy and cost efficiency are priorities. Just be aware that if you're working on large-scale productions, the local-only processing might not cut it—this isn't Adobe Premiere. Still, for quick edits and experimentation, it’s a no-brainer.
